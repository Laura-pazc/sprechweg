The problem

Most language apps are a solo screen habit: swipe a card, keep a streak, never leave the couch. The actual skill gap shows up somewhere else entirely: at the Ausländerbehörde counter, at the doctor's reception, mid-sentence with a running club stranger.

Language sticks when it's used with other people, outside, for a reason that matters that day, not rehearsed alone against a deck of cards.

What it's for

Get a German learner in Hamburg through one real errand in German, and leave them with proof it happened: what they said, what they still need to review, a reason to do the next one.

A mission isn't a lesson. It's a situated task: placement, prep, the real thing outside, then reflection that turns into review.

How it works

the mission loop
01

Evaluate

The level chat

A short chat, five answers, places the learner as beginner, intermediate, or advanced. The tier doesn't change the missions themselves, which stay fixed and authored. It just decides which one shows up first, and how much English scaffolding stays on screen.

02

Prepare

Missions

Vocabulary in context, a conversation simulation, then a practice quiz (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, write-your-own-sentence) before anyone leaves the house.

Live missions: doctor's appointment · Ausländerbehörde · bank errand · running club · yoga class · plus a seasonal set (mushroom foraging, farmers' market, apple cider tasting).
After the vocab step, the app also points to real nearby places to use those same words that day: a café, a U-Bahn stop, a park, shown on a map instead of just a word list.
03

Go outside

Do the errand

The actual appointment, counter, or class, in German, with a real person. The app's job here is to get out of the way, on purpose.

04

Reflect

Field Notes

A short write-up of what happened, a recall check on the vocabulary, a streak, and a badge for the mission. (Called "Journal" internally; renamed to make clear it's evidence the errand got done, not a diary.)

Coming soon · not built this round

Smart Missions: generated from the learner's own Field Notes, not hand-authored

Every mission today is hand-authored and identical for every learner. The planned step replaces that with missions the AI writes on the fly, per learner, out of what that learner actually got wrong — so the library stops being one fixed set and starts being user-generated.

ReadThe Field Note

The AI reads what a learner actually wrote after their errand and flags the real mistakes this particular person keeps making, not a generic grammar check.

GenerateA Smart Mission

Turns those mistakes into a brand-new mission or classic exercise, authored by AI and shaped around this person's errors, this week, in Hamburg — not picked from a fixed catalog.

Missions and Field Notes already run through the same data layer for exactly this reason: it's the seam a backend can plug into later without touching either screen.

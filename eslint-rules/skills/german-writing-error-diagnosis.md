# German Writing: Error-Pattern Diagnosis & Level Detection (A1–C1)

Applies to connected text (a sentence or more) — not single vocabulary items.

## Core principle: cluster, don't flag every instance

**Never comment on every individual error.** Read the whole text first. Group what you find into _error categories_ (patterns that recur), not a list of every occurrence. A text with 15 mistakes that are really "3 categories, repeated" should be presented as 3 categories — because that's what's actually wrong with the learner's internal grammar, and it's what's fixable.

## Core principle: never give the solution

For every flagged category, point to **where** the error is and **what kind** of error it is (e.g. "Kasus nach 'mit'?", "Wortstellung im Nebensatz?", "Verb an Position 2?") — never the corrected form itself, even embedded in an example sentence used to illustrate the rule. The learner must produce the correction. This applies to every level, not just advanced learners.

## Step 1 — Read the whole text before annotating

Don't correct inline as you go. Form the full picture first, then decide which categories are worth naming (see Step 3 on prioritization).

## Step 2 — Match errors against the CEFR pattern table below

Identify which typical patterns for which levels appear, and how consistently. A B1-level learner's text will show mastery of A1/A2 patterns and inconsistent handling of B1 patterns, with B2/C1 patterns largely absent or avoided.

## Step 3 — Cluster and prioritize

- Group same-category errors together, regardless of where they appear in the text.
- If a lower-level pattern (e.g. basic verb-second word order) is still failing, flag that before higher-level patterns — foundational errors matter more for level placement and for the learner's own priorities.
- Don't overload: pick the categories that are most frequent or most diagnostic, not every technically-flaggable thing.

## Step 4 — Estimate the level

State a level (or range, e.g. "solidly B2, reaching into C1") based on:

- The highest level at which patterns are **consistently correct**, not just attempted.
- Presence of avoidance strategies (e.g. only simple main clauses, no subordination) as a sign the learner is below the level their vocabulary might suggest.
- Register and idiomaticity (natural connectors, collocations, Konjunktiv I in indirect speech) as C1-specific signals — these are about _appropriateness_, not just correctness.

## Step 5 — Output format

For each error category found:

- **Category name** (short, e.g. "Nebensatz-Wortstellung")
- **Where it occurs** (quote the fragment, not the whole sentence)
- **What to check** (a guiding question, not the fix) — e.g. "Wo steht das konjugierte Verb im Nebensatz?"
- Do **not** supply the corrected sentence.

Close with:

- **Overall level estimate** + 1-2 sentence justification
- Optional: which category, if fixed, would raise the level fastest (highest-leverage first)

## Step 6 — Mixed practice (optional, on request)

If asked to drill a flagged category: give 3-5 short, complete sentences (not isolated words) that require that pattern. After several single-category rounds, a mixed exercise combining 2+ categories is more realistic and should come after the individual rules are solid, not before.

## CEFR error-pattern reference table

Use this to place errors and to judge what's "expected" vs. "diagnostic" at a given level. A pattern listed at a level is one where errors are common and _forgivable_ below that level, but should be consistently correct at or above it.

| Level  | Typical still-developing patterns                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A1** | Verb-second word order in main clauses; basic article/gender assignment (der/die/das); present tense conjugation endings; basic plural forms                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **A2** | Dative vs. accusative case after prepositions; separable verb placement (verb stem + prefix at clause end); Perfekt formation (haben/sein + Partizip II); basic subordinate clauses with "weil"/"dass"                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **B1** | Subordinate clause word order more broadly (wenn, obwohl, während); comparative/superlative forms; simple Konjunktiv II (würde + Infinitiv); modal verb + infinitive constructions; sentence fragments (subject or verb dropped, especially in wenn-clauses)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **B2** | Adjective endings across declension types (strong/weak/mixed); passive voice (present and Präteritum) with correct subject-verb agreement; fixed verb + preposition government (leiden an/unter, sich freuen auf/über); Konjunktiv II with modal + main verb (only one gets conjugated); relative clauses with prepositions                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **C1** | Konjunktiv I for indirect speech in written register; nuanced connector choice and correct word order per connector type (Hauptsatz- vs. Nebensatz-Konnektoren — e.g. "dennoch" = Position 2, "während"/"obwohl" = verb-final); separable verbs inside relative clauses; register distinctions between spoken and written German; dass/das confusion (orthographic, but persists at this level under time pressure); comma placement after a single fronted clause element vs. a full subordinate clause |

Notes carried over from prior sessions:

- Kasus + adjective endings together are usually the single densest error category at B2/C1 — check this first.
- "dass/das" errors are a spelling/homophone issue, not a grammar gap — don't let it inflate the level estimate downward.
- A learner who avoids subordination entirely (only simple sentences) may look cleaner than they are — absence of errors isn't the same as mastery; flag under-attempted structures too.

## Ways of working

- Minimal, directive communication — no unnecessary preamble.
- Self-correction over direct answers — this is the single most important constraint.
- Don't overload output with exhaustive detail; prioritize what's diagnostic or C1-relevant.
- If corrections are meant for print/handwriting practice rather than typed retyping, say so only if asked — don't assume the delivery format.
